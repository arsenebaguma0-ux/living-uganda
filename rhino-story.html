LIFE / MAMMALS / A HISTORY IN PROGRESS

THE RHINO
STORY.

Loss. Survival. A different beginning.

Uganda’s original rhinos and the southern white rhinos introduced for a new conservation chapter.

Follow the story ↓
START WITH THE DISTINCTION

One story.
Different rhinos.

Uganda’s indigenous rhinos included the northern white rhino and eastern black rhino. The southern white rhinos introduced in the modern recovery programme are a different subspecies from Uganda’s former northern white population. [2]

The northern white rhino is functionally extinct: the International Rhino Foundation reports two surviving females. That is different from having no living individuals anywhere. [1]

UGANDA / A DOCUMENTED TIMELINE

From disappearance
to new beginnings.

  1. Before local disappearance

    Northern white and eastern black rhinos were indigenous to Uganda. [2]

  2. Early 1980s / loss in Uganda

    Conflict, poaching and habitat pressures contributed to their disappearance. UWA’s older overview gives 1982; its January 2026 account identifies a last rhino killed in Kidepo’s Narus Valley in 1983. This date difference is retained for further historical checking. [2] [3]

  3. 2005–2006 / Ziwa

    The modern programme began with southern white rhinos imported from Kenya and the United States. Ziwa provided a protected breeding setting. [2] [3]

  4. January 2026 / Ajai

    UWA reported moving four southern white rhinos from Ziwa to Ajai Wildlife Reserve. [3]

  5. 17 March 2026 / Kidepo

    UWA announced the first two southern white rhinos being moved from Ziwa to Kidepo, within a planned eight-animal programme. Sanctuary infrastructure and monitoring support the move. Planned totals are not treated as completed transfers. [4]

MEET THE SOUTHERN WHITE RHINO

A life shaped
by grass.

Ceratotherium simum simum is the southern subspecies of the white rhinoceros. White rhinos graze in African savannahs; their broad, square upper lip is adapted for feeding on grass. Females and younger animals can be social, while adult males are often solitary and territorial. [1]

Conservation, with context.

The International Rhino Foundation lists white rhinos as Near Threatened on the IUCN Red List and identifies poaching as their greatest threat. This is a species-level global summary, not a Uganda national assessment. The underlying assessment date has not yet been verified here. [1]

Uganda’s introduced southern white rhinos are part of a managed recovery programme; their presence does not mean the former northern white population has been restored.
